## 2. Card Formulation Rules (Specific to MBB 321 — Calibrated to Mara's Study Style & Exam Mastery)

### A. Biochemical Reactions: The "Equation Cloze Rule"
- **Never** test an entire reaction equation as a single massive blank (`{{c1::A + B -> C + D}}`).
- **Never** waste a cloze testing arbitrary step index numbers (`Step {{c1::6}}`).
- Format every pathway reaction as a visual equation on a single note with **2 or 3 coordinated functional cloze groups**:
  - **`c1` (Carbon Substrates & Products):** Main carbon substrate(s) and product(s)
  - **`c2` (Catalyst / Enzyme):** Enzyme name inside brackets over the arrow: `——[{{c2::Enzyme}}]——>`
  - **`c3` (or `c2` for 2-tier simplicity — Energetics / Cofactors):** Energy carriers, coenzymes, and inorganic molecules (`ATP`, `ADP`, `NAD⁺`, `NADH + H⁺`, `FAD`, `FADH₂`, `HS-CoA`, `Pi`, `CO₂`, `H₂O`)

**Standard Reaction Template:**
```html
[Pathway / Step Prefix]:<br>{{c1::Substrate}} + {{c3::HS-CoA + NAD⁺}} ——[{{c2::Enzyme}}]——> {{c1::Product}} + {{c3::NADH + H⁺}}
```

### B. Unbundling Multi-Step Enzyme Cascades (1 Partial Reaction = 1 Card)
- **Never cram a multi-step enzymatic cascade into one run-on sentence card!**
  - ❌ *Bad:* Putting E1 decarboxylation, E1/E2 transacetylation, E2 transesterification, and E3 reoxidation all into one giant sentence with 8 clozes.
  - ✅ *Good:*
    1. **Parent Subunit Card (Abbreviation `c1` vs. Full Name `c2`):**
       `"The Pyruvate Dehydrogenase (PDH) complex is composed of three catalytic subunits: {{c1::E1}} ({{c2::pyruvate dehydrogenase}}), {{c1::E2}} ({{c2::dihydrolipoyl transacetylase}}), and {{c1::E3}} ({{c2::dihydrolipoyl dehydrogenase}})."`
    2. **Sequential Step Cards (1 Step = 1 Card, each with its slide diagram in `extra`):**
       - Step 1 (E1): `"In the PDH reaction sequence, {{c1::E1}} accepts a two-carbon aldehyde group from pyruvate and binds it to {{c2::TPP}} to form {{c2::hydroxyethyl-TPP (HETPP)}}, releasing one carbon as {{c1::CO₂}}."`
       - Step 2 (E1 → E2): `"In the PDH reaction sequence, {{c1::E1}} transfers the {{c2::acetyl}} group from TPP to the {{c2::lipoamide swinging arm}} on {{c1::E2}}, forming an {{c2::acetyl-lipoyl thioester}}."`
       - Step 3 (E2): `"In the PDH reaction sequence, {{c1::E2}} transfers the {{c2::acetyl}} group to {{c2::HS-CoA}} to produce {{c2::acetyl-CoA}}, leaving {{c1::lipoamide}} in its reduced ({{c1::dihydrolipoyl}}) state."`
       - Step 4 (E3 Part 1): `"In the PDH reaction sequence, {{c1::E3}} reoxidizes {{c2::reduced dihydrolipoyl}} by transferring two electrons/hydrogens to its immobile cofactor {{c2::FAD}} to form {{c2::FADH₂}}."`
       - Step 5 (E3 Part 2): `"In the PDH E3 subunit, reduced {{c1::FADH₂}} transfers a hydride to mobile {{c2::NAD⁺}} to generate {{c2::NADH + H⁺}}."`

### C. Natural Subject-First Sentence Structure & Atomic Clozes (No "Jeopardy-Style" Paragraph Clozes)
- **Write in natural Subject → Predicate order:** Never start a card by clozing a 15–25 word definition clause (`"{{c2::Serving as an activated acyl-group carrier...}} is the primary function of {{c1::Coenzyme A}}"`). Nobody can recall a 20-word clause verbatim in Anki!
- Instead, put the term at the subject position (`c2`) and cloze the short, 1–4 word high-yield functional phrases (`c1`):
  - ✅ *Benchmark Example:* `"{{c2::Coenzyme A (CoA)}} is an activated {{c1::acyl-group}} carrier with a high transfer potential that attaches acyl groups to its terminal {{c1::thiol (-SH)}} group."`
  - ✅ *Benchmark Example:* `"The PDH complex operates via a {{c1::Ping-Pong}} kinetic mechanism classified as a {{c2::tri-tri (3 substrates, 3 products)}} reaction."`

### D. 2-Tier Mutual-Cue Cloze Hygiene (Avoid Both "All-`c1` Blackout" and "9-Cloze Overload")
- **Prefer 2 Cloze Tiers (`c1` and `c2`) on conceptual/mechanistic cards:** Group parallel structural/entity terms in `c1` and functional/mechanistic outcomes in `c2` so `c1` cues `c2` and `c2` cues `c1`.
- **Max 3–4 Cloze Boxes per Card:** Never place 5–9 cloze boxes in a single sentence. If a slide has 6–8 testable facts, split them across 2 concise sequential cards on that slide.
- **No Adjacent Cognate Giveaways:** Never place root-related words in adjacent clozes (e.g., never write `the {{c1::carboxylate}} group {{c2::decarboxylates}}` where the visible word gives away the hidden blank).

### E. Mandatory Pathway / Subunit Context Prefix
- Because flashcards are reviewed in shuffled order across the entire semester, every mechanistic card must begin with a concise context prefix (e.g., `PDH (E1 Decarboxylation): ...`, `PDH (E1 → E2 Transfer): ...`, `PDH (E2 Transesterification): ...`, `TCA Step 0: ...`). Never start a card with an unanchored pronoun or generic action (`"Deprotonation of the hydroxy group..."`) without stating which enzyme/step it belongs to.

### F. Qualitative Thermodynamics & Orbital/Chemical Rationale Over Raw Decimals
- **Test the "Why" and Directionality, Not Raw Decimals:** Cloze whether a step is `{{c1::exergonic}}`/`endergonic` and `{{c1::irreversible}}`/`reversible`, along with the chemical or orbital rationale:
  - ✅ *Benchmark Example:* `"In an acetyl-thioester, the carbonyl carbon has an oxidation state of {{c1::+3}} (identical to an O-ester), but Acetyl-CoA hydrolysis is strongly {{c2::exergonic}} (ΔG′° = –29.6 kJ/mol) because the {{c1::C–S}} bond is {{c2::weaker and less resonance-stabilized}} than a {{c1::C–O}} bond."`
  - ✅ *Benchmark Example:* `"Thioesters have a high group-transfer potential because sulfur's larger {{c1::3p}} orbitals overlap {{c2::poorly}} with carbon's {{c1::2p}} orbitals, preventing effective {{c2::electron delocalization (resonance)}} into the carbonyl."`
- Keep exact $\Delta G'^\circ$ decimal numbers (`–29.6 kJ/mol`, `–33.5 kJ/mol`) un-clozed in parentheses as helpful context unless the slide specifically contrasts energy-tier benchmarks (such as phosphoanhydride $\approx -7.3\text{ kcal/mol}$ vs. phosphoester $\approx -3.3\text{ kcal/mol}$).

### G. Zero Historical Trivia, Zero Recap Duplicates, and Slide Typo Fact-Checking
- **No Historical Trivia:** Never generate cards on scientist names, discovery dates, or Nobel Prizes (e.g., Fritz Lipmann or Hans Krebs winning the 1953 Nobel Prize).
- **No End-of-Lecture Summary Duplicates:** When the final slide of a deck is a recap/summary of concepts already tested on Slides 1–14, do not generate redundant summary cards at the end of the deck.
- **Biochemical Fact-Checking on Slide Typos:** If a lecture slide contains an obvious biochemical typo (e.g., stating that $\text{NAD}^+$ is derived from Vitamin B12 instead of **Vitamin B3 / niacin**), always test the **biochemically correct fact** (`{{c2::Vitamin B3 (niacin)}}`) on the card front and mention the slide typo in `extra`.

### H. Chronological Slide Sequencing & Image/Clarification-Only `extra` Field
- **Strict Slide Sequence:** Generate cards in strict ascending slide order (`Slide 1`, then `Slide 2`, then `Slide 3`, etc.). Never jump back and forth between slides.
- **100% Testable Coverage Check:** Audit every slide so all testable material (reactions, reaction types, enzymes, cofactors, catalytic residues, qualitative thermodynamics, and allosteric regulators) is directly tested inside `{{c1::...}}`/`{{c2::...}}` clozes in `text`.
- **Image & Clarification-Only `extra`:** The `extra` field must ONLY contain the embedded slide diagram (`<img src="...">`) and/or a brief 1-sentence visual pointer or non-testable clarification. NEVER place untested reactions, reaction classifications, regulators, or catalytic mechanisms in `extra`—spawn a dedicated card on that slide instead!
